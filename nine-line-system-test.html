How the score is calculated

Each trial’s increase above background is capped at 30 dB. The 5-, 10- and 20-foot tests contribute 20%, 30% and 50%. The weighted increase maps to 1–10; near-clipping subtracts up to 2 points, reaching that maximum at 5% weighted near-clipping. Missing or invalid recordings show insufficient data. Very quiet levels use a −100 dBFS floor for this approximate score. This is not a word-clarity or noise-classification score.

Average and peak recording levels are in dBFS: values closer to zero mean a stronger digital signal. The background comparison is the difference from the room-noise recording, not a speech-to-noise ratio.

These are sampled digital audio levels, not calibrated sound-pressure levels (dB SPL), word clarity, usable range or certified device compatibility. Keep your speaking volume and phone position consistent.

Results and privacy limits

Only the measured 5-, 10- and 20-foot distances are tested. No result is inferred for other distances. Results are digital audio-level measurements, not verified native 9/Line compatibility scores, and apply only to the distances and conditions tested. Browser capture can differ from 9/Line’s native audio pipeline. This tests the microphone; speaker and earpiece tests are separate.

Recordings stay in memory and disappear on reload or close. No audio uploads, recording downloads, persistent recording history or analytics. No speech model or transcript is used. Score submission is currently disabled. Hosting infrastructure processes ordinary connection information.

Microphone access needs HTTPS and browser support. Open this page directly if an embedded preview blocks it.
