How it works

From dial to dialogue.

The exact workflow depends on agency configuration. This page separates the standard call path from policy choices and network-dependent behavior.

The standard call flow

01

Authorized device

An officer or responder calls from a telephone number registered to the agency.

→
02

Start #919

The caller reaches the agency’s configured EMT919 entry point and identifies the needed language or situation.

→
03

Turn-by-turn

The responder states what needs to be communicated. EMT919 interprets each turn while preserving speaker roles.

→
04

Escalate when needed

The session can move to a live interpreter according to agency policy, caller request, or detected difficulty.

Language selection and timing

The caller identifies the language or begins the configured interaction. EMT919 does not publish a fixed response-latency figure. Connection and response time vary with carrier and network conditions, language, routing mode, audio quality, and live-interpreter availability.

Live-interpreter boundary

AI-first mode is intended to begin communication quickly. It does not eliminate professional interpreters. Agencies may choose live-interpreter-only routing, and policy should require a qualified live interpreter for legal, medical, disciplinary, or other high-stakes communications whenever appropriate.

If the call disconnects

The active session ends. The responder can call again through the authorized workflow or use the agency’s alternate interpreter path. Session continuity and record availability may depend on where the interruption occurred.

Audio, transcripts, and retention

  • Voice audio: 30-day rolling deletion by default; a shorter period may be configured.
  • Transcripts: retained for the agency agreement by default; agencies may select a shorter rolling period.
  • Access and audit logs: retained for 12 months.
  • Customer content: not used to train AI models.
  • Legal or agency preservation holds: excluded from ordinary deletion until released.
