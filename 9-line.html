BEFORE YOU GET STARTED

Questions worth asking.

Is 9-Line the same as dialing #919?

No. 9-Line is the name of the officer calling and messaging product. Do not assume that dialing #919 launches 9-Line or that the two services have identical access requirements.

Does every officer get a separate number?

Yes. Each officer receives a dedicated 9-Line number for calls and SMS/MMS. Use that number for work contacts to keep the officer's personal phone number private.

Can residents call or text the officer back?

Yes. 9-Line supports incoming calls and two-way SMS/MMS at the officer's dedicated number. It is a follow-up contact channel, not a continuously monitored emergency line.

Does 9-Line translate calls and messages?

9-Line provides officer calling and messaging. EMT919 interpretation is a separate language-access offering. Automatic translation is not included among the capabilities described here; discuss any integration requirements with the team. Explore EMT919 interpretation.

Are calls recorded and messages retained?

Recording, storage, retention, export, and access terms must be confirmed specifically for 9-Line in the agency's agreement. This page does not promise automatic recording or a particular retention period.

How does an agency get started?

Request a briefing with your approximate officer count and intended calling and messaging uses. Availability, onboarding, service configuration, and commercial terms are confirmed with the EMT919 team. Do not send case details or protected information.

Not a replacement for 911, dispatch, or radio.

Use your agency's designated emergency and dispatch channels for urgent response. Do not assume a call or message has been received, read, or monitored.
