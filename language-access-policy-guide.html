Policy guide

Define the handoff before the call.

Questions agencies can use when reviewing where automated interpretation may begin and where qualified live interpretation is required.

Policy decisions to document

  • Which roles and registered telephone numbers may use the service?
  • Which contact types are approved for AI-first interpretation?
  • Which matters require a qualified live interpreter from the outset?
  • What words, signals, or circumstances trigger escalation?
  • How should a responder proceed if the call disconnects or quality is poor?
  • What records may be retained, who may access them, and for how long?
  • How will accessibility, privacy, legal, records, labor, and clinical stakeholders review the workflow?
  • How will field users be trained and recurrently evaluated?
  • How will quality concerns and incidents be reported?

Suggested evaluation

Test representative languages, background noise, overlapping speech, proper nouns, critical commands, phone placement, live escalation, disconnection, and unavailable service. Record expected behavior and approval criteria before deployment.

This guide does not create a policy or determine legal compliance. Agencies should use their own counsel, language-access coordinator, records officer, clinical leadership, and command staff.
